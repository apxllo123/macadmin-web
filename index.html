01 · The actual problem

Why a standard account creates friction

A standard macOS account is intentionally limited. Apple says standard users can install apps and change their own settings, but they cannot add users or change other users' settings. Apple also says that some Mac tasks require an administrator name and password.

The argument is not “every coding task needs admin.”The stronger and more accurate claim is that a development machine sometimes needs system-level installation or configuration, and a standard account can make those legitimate tasks depend on another person being available.

03 · Concrete development reasons

Where administrator authorization can be relevant

My workflow is broader than writing code: I am doing substantial local builds, tests, tooling, and development-environment work.

Heavy local testing

Medusa and VEX involve real local development, builds, and testing. The tests themselves do not automatically require admin; the surrounding toolchain sometimes can.

VEX + ChatWise MCP

VEX uses local AI tooling and ChatWise MCP integration. I need a locally managed MCP development environment that I can configure and troubleshoot myself.

System-level development setup

Developer components, runtimes, system configuration, and installations can require elevated authorization even when ordinary coding and tests do not.

Why this is stronger than “I code.”The request points to a real development workload plus documented system-level development operations. I am not claiming that every command needs admin.
04 · The actual request

What I need admin for vs. what I do not

This is deliberately split so I am not exaggerating the technical requirements.

What I need administrator access for

  • Installing and configuring developer tools when elevated authorization is genuinely required.
  • Managing system-level development components and configuration.
  • Running and troubleshooting large local build and test environments without waiting for someone else to supply credentials.
  • Setting up and maintaining my local ChatWise/MCP development environment when system-level changes are required.
  • Independently troubleshooting installation and configuration problems on the Mac I use for development.
The key point: administrator access removes a handoff when the operating system requires elevated authorization; it does not make every development task privileged.

What I do not need admin for

  • Writing ordinary source code.
  • Running every normal test command.
  • Claiming that every .dmg download or installation requires admin.
  • Claiming that administrator access is incapable of affecting parental controls.
  • Creating extra accounts to get around family rules.
  • Deliberately disabling Screen Time or other parental controls.
Why this matters: I am asking for a development capability, not a claim that admin access is harmless or unlimited.

06 · Honesty about the risk

I am not asking you to believe something false

  • I am not claiming administrator access is impossible to misuse.
  • I am not claiming Screen Time cannot be affected by administrator-level changes.
  • I am not claiming every developer tool requires admin access.
  • I will not create additional accounts to get around family rules.
  • I will not deliberately change parental-control settings to defeat agreed limits.
Apple's warning is real.Apple explicitly recommends a standard account for a child because an administrator can make changes affecting all accounts. That is why this proposal uses rules and accountability instead of denying the technical reality.
07 · What this evidence does not prove

The boundary of the argument

Not proved
“Admin cannot bypass Screen Time.”

The evidence does not justify this statement.

Not proved
“Every .dmg requires admin.”

A disk image is not itself an administrator requirement; installation behavior depends on the software.

Supported
“Some Mac tasks require administrator authorization.”

Apple explicitly documents this.

Supported
“Some Apple development setup involves privileged operations.”

Apple's Command Line Tools and Xcode documentation provide concrete examples.

08 · Proposed agreement

A fair way to handle the trust issue

1

Administrator access

Let me manage my personally purchased Mac and complete legitimate development setup.

2

No extra accounts

I will not create additional profiles for the purpose of getting around family rules.

3

No deliberate control changes

I will not deliberately change Screen Time, Family Sharing, or parental-control settings to defeat agreed limits.

4

Keep the password private

The administrator password stays private; I am asking for the account privilege, not the parent's password.

5

Accountability

If I deliberately violate the agreement, we revisit or remove administrator access.

“I am not asking you to ignore the risk. I am asking you to let me use the computer I bought as a real development machine, with clear rules, primary-source evidence, and a consequence if I abuse the privilege.”
